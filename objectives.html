Course Objectives & Learning Outcomes

The Principles of Cryptography Virtual Laboratory provides hands-on experimental reinforcement of theoretical cryptography concepts taught in undergraduate and graduate computer science programs.

Each laboratory experiment is structured around measurable competencies mapped to Bloom's Revised Taxonomy, bridging formal mathematics with interactive algorithm visualization.

Measurable Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of all virtual lab modules, students will be able to:

CO 1
Explain Substitution and Transposition Mechanisms:

Distinguish between confusion-based character substitution and diffusion-based positional permutation in historical ciphers.

CO 2
Perform Caesar, Playfair, and Hill Cipher Transformations:

Compute Caesar modular shifts, generate 5x5 Playfair keyword matrices with digraph rules, and execute Hill cipher matrix vector multiplications modulo 26 with determinant invertibility checks.

CO 3
Execute Row-Major and Column-Major Transpositions:

Model zigzag wave paths across arbitrary rail depths and perform rectangular grid-based column-major transformations and inverse reconstructions.

CO 4
Understand the DES and AES Encryption Workflows:

Trace the 16 Feistel rounds, S-box non-linearities, and subkey schedules of DES, and analyze the 128-bit state transformations of AES (SubBytes, ShiftRows, MixColumns, AddRoundKey) and AES-GCM authenticated encryption.

CO 5
Demonstrate RSA Asymmetric Encryption and Decryption:

Calculate modulus n, Euler totient phi(n), coprime public exponent e, and modular inverse private exponent d, executing repeated-squaring modular exponentiation and modern RSA-OAEP.

CO 6
Explain Diffie–Hellman Key Exchange and Man-in-the-Middle Risks:

Simulate Alice and Bob establishing symmetric shared secrets over an unauthenticated channel, and analyze how an active adversary can intercept public parameters without digital certificates.

CO 7
Calculate and Interpret SHA-1 and MD5 Message Digests:

Evaluate one-way hashing, observe the Avalanche Effect bit distributions, and explain collision vulnerabilities (SHAttered, Wang et al.).

CO 8
Master Digital Signature Generation and Verification:

Execute Digital Signature Standard (DSS / DSA) signing and verification equations, and demonstrate how modifying a message payload causes immediate verification rejection.

CO 9
Identify the Security Limitations of Historical Algorithms:

Articulate why DES, MD5, SHA-1, and single-parameter Caesar ciphers must be categorized as obsolete/educational, while recommending modern standards (AES-GCM, SHA-256, RSA-OAEP, ECDSA).
