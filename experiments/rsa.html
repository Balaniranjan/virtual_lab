EXPERIMENT 05 Asymmetric Cryptography

RSA Encryption Algorithm: Number Theoretic Foundations & RSA-OAEP

Investigate public-key cryptography via prime number generation, Euler's totient function, modular multiplicative inverse derivation, square-and-multiply exponentiation, and practical 2048-bit RSA-OAEP.

Pedagogical Distinction
Classroom Notice: Textbook RSA (direct arithmetic c = m^e mod n without padding) is strictly a mathematical teaching model. In production systems, unpadded RSA is malleable and vulnerable; real applications mandate randomized padding (RSA-OAEP) and key sizes of 2048+ bits.

Aim of the Experiment

To demonstrate and analyze the mathematical and computational principles of the RSA public-key algorithm, calculating the modulus, Euler's totient, public and private exponents, executing modular exponentiation via repeated squaring, and contrasting textbook RSA with modern 2048-bit RSA-OAEP encryption.

Key Learning Outcomes

  • Validate prime numbers p and q and compute the composite modulus n = p * q.
  • Calculate Euler's totient phi(n) = (p - 1)(q - 1) and find a coprime public exponent e.
  • Compute the modular multiplicative inverse d = e^-1 mod phi(n) using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm.
  • Perform square-and-multiply modular exponentiation for encryption (c = m^e mod n) and decryption (m = c^d mod n).
  • Execute modern 2048-bit RSA-OAEP encryption and keypair export via the Web Crypto API.

Theoretical Principles of RSA

1. Mathematical Foundation (Euler & Fermat)

Published in 1977 by Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman, RSA is grounded in Euler's Generalization of Fermat's Little Theorem:

Euler's Theorem: If gcd(a, n) = 1, then a^{\phi(n)} ≡ 1 \pmod{n}.

By selecting exponents e and d such that e × d ≡ 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}, we establish that:
m^{e \cdot d} = m^{k \cdot \phi(n) + 1} = (m^{\phi(n)})^k \cdot m ≡ (1)^k \cdot m ≡ m \pmod{n}.

2. Step-by-Step RSA Algorithm

  1. Key Generation:
    • Select two distinct large primes p and q.
    • Compute modulus n = p × q.
    • Compute Euler's totient \phi(n) = (p - 1)(q - 1).
    • Select public exponent e such that 1 < e < \phi(n) and gcd(e, \phi(n)) = 1 (commonly 65537).
    • Compute private exponent d = e^{-1} \pmod{\phi(n)} such that (d × e) \bmod \phi(n) = 1 using the Extended Euclidean Algorithm.
    • Public Key: (e, n) | Private Key: (d, n).
  2. Encryption:
    Given message integer m < n, calculate ciphertext: c = m^e \bmod n.
  3. Decryption:
    Given ciphertext c, calculate original message: m = c^d \bmod n.

3. Why Padding (OAEP) is Mandatory in Practice

In textbook RSA, if an attacker encrypts two plaintexts m_1 and m_2, the product of their ciphertexts equals the encryption of their product: E(m_1) × E(m_2) = (m_1^e)(m_2^e) \bmod n = (m_1 m_2)^e \bmod n = E(m_1 m_2).

This multiplicative homomorphic property makes textbook RSA malleable and insecure against chosen-ciphertext attacks (IND-CCA2). Optimal Asymmetric Encryption Padding (RSA-OAEP) introduces two cryptographic hash steps and a random seed, transforming deterministic RSA into a semantically secure, non-malleable scheme.

Simulation Procedure

  1. Navigate to the Practice tab.
  2. Section 1: Mathematical Classroom Demo:
    • Enter two small distinct primes p and q (e.g. p=61, q=53 or p=17, q=11).
    • Specify or auto-generate a valid coprime exponent e.
    • Click Generate Key Parameters. Observe the calculated n, \phi(n), and private exponent d.
    • Enter a message integer m < n and click Encrypt Number.
    • Trace the square-and-multiply binary exponentiation steps in the visualizer panel.
    • Click Decrypt Number to verify that c^d \bmod n recovers m.
  3. Section 2: Practical Web Crypto RSA-OAEP:
    • Click Generate 2048-bit Keypair. Inspect the exported SPKI public key and PKCS8 private key PEM blocks.
    • Enter an arbitrary text message and click Encrypt (RSA-OAEP).
    • Observe the resulting Base64 and Hex ciphertext.
    • Click Decrypt (RSA-OAEP) to verify authenticated decryption.
