EXPERIMENT 02 Classical Cryptography

Transposition Techniques: Row-Major and Column-Major Rail Fence Ciphers

Analyze permutation-based cryptography by comparing the diagonal zigzag wave traversal of Row-Major Rail Fence with the rectangular grid permutation of Column-Major Transposition.

Educational Legacy Primitive
Cryptographic Notice: Transposition ciphers preserve 100% of natural language letter frequencies. They are easily cracked using anagramming and bigram reconstruction, and serve here strictly to teach the concept of diffusion.

Aim of the Experiment

To explore the principles of permutation-based encryption by modeling, simulating, and analyzing the character placement grids, reading sequences, and inverse reconstruction mechanics of Row-Major (Zigzag) and Column-Major (Rectangular Grid) Transposition Ciphers.

Key Learning Outcomes

  • Understand the distinction between substitution (confusion) and transposition (diffusion).
  • Trace the diagonal wave pattern of a zigzag rail fence across arbitrary rail counts (R ≥ 2).
  • Execute the rectangular grid convention of Column-Major transposition and handle incomplete columns.
  • Verify that letter frequency distributions remain invariant under pure transposition.

Theoretical Principles of Transposition

1. Transposition vs. Substitution

Unlike substitution ciphers where characters change their identities, transposition ciphers rearrange the order or positional coordinates of the plaintext characters according to a regular geometric or permutation system.

In Shannon's terminology, substitution introduces Confusion (obscuring the link between the key and the ciphertext), while transposition introduces Diffusion (spreading statistical redundancies of the plaintext over wide spans of ciphertext).

2. Rail Fence: Row-Major (Zigzag Wave Convention)

In the classical Row-Major rail fence cipher, characters are written along an alternating diagonal wave across R parallel horizontal rails:

Path Mechanics:

Starting at rail 0, characters advance diagonally downward to rail R - 1, then reverse direction and advance diagonally upward to rail 0, repeating cyclically with period T = 2(R - 1).

Ciphertext Generation:

Once the plaintext is written along the zigzag path, the ciphertext is generated by reading out each rail horizontally from left to right, beginning at rail 0 and ending at rail R - 1.

Decryption:

To invert the transformation, the receiver marks the empty zigzag slots for a message of length L, fills the marked slots row-by-row with ciphertext characters, and finally reads the grid along the original zigzag wave path.

3. Rail Fence: Column-Major (Rectangular Grid Convention)

To avoid confusion between different historical conventions, our laboratory establishes an explicit, documented Column-Major Grid Convention:

Grid Layout Convention:

  1. Plaintext is written horizontally row-by-row into a rectangular matrix having C columns (where C = number of rails/columns).
  2. The required number of rows is rows = ceil(Length / C).
  3. Ciphertext is produced by reading the matrix vertically, column-by-column, starting from column 0 through column C - 1.

Handling Incomplete Columns during Decryption:

If the message length L is not a multiple of C, the remainder r = L mod C indicates that the first r columns have length rows, while the remaining columns have length rows - 1. The decryption algorithm accurately reconstructs these variable column lengths before reading out the grid row-by-row.

Simulation Procedure

  1. Navigate to the Practice tab.
  2. Select the desired transposition mode: Row-Major (Zigzag) or Column-Major (Grid).
  3. Enter your plaintext message in the input text area.
  4. Select or input the number of rails / columns (minimum 2).
  5. Click Encrypt to visualize character placement across the generated grid.
  6. Observe how row concatenations (Row-Major) or column reads (Column-Major) produce the final ciphertext.
  7. Click Decrypt to observe inverse grid reconstruction and message recovery.
  8. Take the Posttest to confirm mastery of transposition ciphers.

Plaintext: CRYPTOGRAPHY (length 12)

Columns: C = 4 (Rows = 12 / 4 = 3)

Row 0: C  R  Y  P
Row 1: T  O  G  R
Row 2: A  P  H  Y

Reading columns:
Col 0: CTA
Col 1: ROP
Col 2: YGH
Col 3: PRY

Ciphertext: CTA ROP YGH PRY -> CTAROPYGHPRY
