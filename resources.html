Learning Resources & Standards

Authentic academic literature, official NIST standards, IETF RFCs, and foundational textbooks for advanced study in cryptography and information security.

Core University Textbooks

Cryptography and Network Security: Principles and Practice (7th/8th Edition)

Author: William Stallings | Publisher: Pearson Education

The definitive reference for classical substitution/transposition techniques, the mathematical structure of DES/AES, public-key algorithms, and digital signature standards.

Handbook of Applied Cryptography

Authors: Alfred J. Menezes, Paul C. van Oorschot, Scott A. Vanstone | Publisher: CRC Press

An authoritative graduate-level treatise offering exhaustive mathematical treatments of modular arithmetic, finite fields, discrete logarithms, and provable security. Available free online under academic distribution.

Cryptography Engineering: Design Principles and Practical Applications

Authors: Niels Ferguson, Bruce Schneier, Tadayoshi Kohno | Publisher: John Wiley & Sons

Focuses on practical implementation security, explaining why textbook RSA fails, how side-channel attacks operate, and the critical role of authenticated encryption (AEAD).

Network Security: PRIVATE Communication in a PUBLIC World (2nd Edition)

Authors: Charlie Kaufman, Radia Perlman, Mike Speciner | Publisher: Prentice Hall

Provides deep architectural insights into key establishment protocols, Diffie-Hellman vulnerability analysis, certificate chains, and real-world network protocols.

Cryptographic Terminology Glossary

Confusion
A principle introduced by Claude Shannon stating that the relationship between the key and the ciphertext should be as complex and involved as possible, typically implemented via non-linear substitution boxes (S-boxes).
Diffusion
A principle stating that statistical characteristics of the plaintext should be dissipated across the ciphertext, so that changing a single plaintext bit alters many ciphertext bits, typically achieved through permutations and linear mixing.
Avalanche Effect
A cryptographic property wherein a small modification in the input (e.g. flipping a single bit) produces a drastic, uncorrelated change in the output (approximately 50% of the bits flipping).
Collision Resistance
The property of a cryptographic hash function that makes it computationally infeasible to find any two distinct inputs x and y such that H(x) = H(y).
AEAD (Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data)
A modern cryptographic mode (e.g. AES-GCM) that simultaneously provides confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity guarantees in a single primitive.
